# Changelog

## Unreleased

- The drop log (build 0.0.19, for the Raid Desk's drop lists; design `docs/design/drop-list-import.md`, LOOT-008): inside dungeons and raids, every client with the addon counts which creature or encounter dropped which items, and never who looted them. A boss is a creature that was a boss unit of an encounter won there, or (with any loot method: the live client gave no boss units in a dungeon) a corpse opened within 5 minutes of the win that holds an item the game's loot history filed under that win's rolls; a corpse opened before its rolls are filed waits up to 15 seconds for them (kept in the saved data, so a `/reload` loses nothing), the roll and the window of one item count once, and a boss learned this way takes only the items its own corpse held, since its encounter may have more bosses (the rest stay under the encounter); when neither applies, the master looter's first corpse with loot at the threshold within 5 minutes of the win is a guessed boss (in an area-loot window, the corpse with the best item), sure after 2 kills, and a string with DungeonJournal's lists added drops the mark when the journal names that creature; a chest counts for the master looter within those 5 minutes; an encounter with several bosses keeps its rolls and chest under the encounter, not under one of them; a rare is a creature the game classed rare (seen as the target or under the mouse) and stays one; everything else is trash. The windows of the player's own Skinning, Mining, Herb Gathering and Pick Pocket are not drops. Kept: Uncommon and better from bosses and rares, Rare and better from trash, quest and bind-on-equip items flagged; each corpse counted once (also after a /reload), a roll filed under its encounter only when the game's loot history says so. Nothing of a player is kept: no names, no GUIDs (a corpse is an 8-character checksum), no roll results. Caps: 80 items per creature (an item seen more than once is never dropped for a new one), 100 creatures per zone, 60 zones, 20,000 items (a full log drops the zone seen longest ago). `/im drops` opens the drops window with the zones seen in the last 7 days (`list` numbers the zones, `all`, `1 3`, `dj <dungeon>` for DungeonJournal's list of one dungeon), and a box adds DungeonJournal's lists. The string is the new kind `drops` (`IM1:drops:...`); the Raid Desk cannot import it yet. The loot master's window now says where an item drops from this client's own loot windows first, then DungeonJournal. What the game answered is counted in `IntrepidManagerDB.drops.stats` (boss units read or secret, rolls filed or not, corpses held and matched, creatures seen or secret, windows with no source), so one dungeon run's file shows what works. New files `core/DropLog.lua`, `core/Drops.lua`, `game/DropWatch.lua`, `ui/DropsWindow.lua`: a full game restart is needed.
- DungeonJournal, read only (build 0.0.18, at the loot owner's request): when the player has the community addon DungeonJournal (folder `ForeverDungeonJournal`, an optional dependency), the loot master's session window says where the picked item drops ("From Boss, Dungeon"), before the start and next to the counts. `/im dj` checks what the journal holds and keeps the counts in `IntrepidManagerDB.dj` (no names); `/im dj list` prints its dungeons. New file `game/DungeonJournal.lua`: it reads the journal's published table, its dungeons as candidate zones (bosses with their shared items, rares, trash pages to the zone-wide list), and never changes anything of the journal's. DungeonJournal is All Rights Reserved: nothing of it is copied here, and the tests use an invented journal. New file: a full game restart is needed.
- Import the night and export the awards (build 0.0.17, so the website can import what the addon exports): `/im import` (or the **Import night** button of the loot master's window) opens the window that takes the night string of the Raid Desk's "Copy for addon" and keeps the night it accepted; `/im export` (or **Export awards**) opens the window with the awards string to paste into the Raid Desk. New file `game/Exchange.lua` records every award of a session in the shape of `docs/spec/exchange-v0.md` section 6 in the saved variables (`IntrepidManagerDB.awards`): assigned when the item is awarded, received (how: trade, or manual for an item the loot master keeps or hands over) when the trade went through, removed with a higher revision when the award is taken back; changing the recipient is a removal and a new award. The ids come from the session start and four random characters of the load, so a crash never reuses one. With a night loaded the awards carry its phase and raid, the roster id of the recipient (a unique full-name match), and the priority of the item (the one the recipient is on when several lists have the item), with the boss; without one they still have everything the spec requires. The export holds the awards the night does not know in that revision (new or changed), "everything" or, with no night, the last 14 days. Disenchant and Guild bank are awards to a pseudo recipient: `/im award <item> disenchant` or `guild bank`, then Trade marks the item as handed over. The string was checked with the reference reader of the tools. The addon still does not show the night's priorities in the council windows. New file: a full game restart is needed.
- Synthetic nights and awards (`tools/synthetic.py`, to practice the copy-and-paste with the Raid Desk on made-up raids): `night` makes a night string (what "Copy for addon" will make) with an invented roster, bosses and priorities, `awards --night FILE` makes the awards string for a night, `pack` writes a whole rehearsal folder (the night, eight awards strings that walk through every row category of the importer preview in the order to paste them, six broken strings that must be refused, the state the app is assumed to hold before each paste, the expected rows, and a README), and `check` reads any pasted string (`--clipboard` takes it from the Mac's clipboard) with the reference reader and says what it holds, and with `--state` what the importer's preview would show. The preview in the tool is a reference for spec section 6.3, not the app: the Raid Desk's importer (LOOT-005) is not built yet. A few of the strings are committed as fixtures (`docs/spec/fixtures/synthetic/`) and the Lua tests read them: the addon's reader finds the same row errors as the reference and its writer makes the same bytes. CI runs `synthetic.py selftest`.
- Award first, then trade what was won (build 0.0.16; after the first completed trade, the loot owner asked that a trade carry only what the raider won): the old `/im trade <item> <name>` gave any item to anyone, and in the test it gave an item the other player had not even answered. Now the loot master awards an item to a raider (an **Award** button next to each answer, or `/im award <item> <name>`; `/im award` alone lists who has won what), the window marks the winner's row and shows "won" in the list, and **Trade** next to the winner (or `/im trade <name>`) opens the trade and puts in everything awarded to that raider, up to six items, one after the other. Nothing else is ever put in their window. The **x** next to the winner (or `/im award clear <item>`) takes an award back until the item is given; when the game says "Trade complete." the items that were in the window at the last accept show as given (the others stay awarded for the next trade). A winner who did not answer gets a row of their own; the loot master can award an item to themself (it stays in the bags and is marked as given). A trade window that is open already is only used when it is with the winner (the game's `UnitName("npc")`, or a window the addon opened itself): the items would otherwise go to the wrong player. New session warns before it drops awards that are not given yet (a second press within 10 seconds does). The loot master's window is 40 pixels wider. No new file: a `/reload` is enough.
- Version check and trade fixes (build 0.0.15): after joining a group every client announces its addon version on the group chat (three seconds after the roster settles, not during a boss fight), and a client with another version answers with its own, so a group of equals stays quiet. `/im versions` and the Versions button of the loot master's window ask everybody and open a window that lists each member of the group with the class picture, the version and "same", "older", "newer", "waiting", "not installed" or "offline" (a member who has not answered five seconds after the question counts as not installed); a newer version than yours is told once in the chat (three lines at most in one login) and the window asks for an update. The hellos of a loot session feed the list too. Versions compare number by number (`core/Version.lua`: 0.0.9 is older than 0.0.14). The council protocol (still the test protocol v1, prefix `IMC1`) has two new messages, `V` (announce) and `Q` (full check); a client on 0.0.14 or older ignores them, so until everyone has this build such a member shows as "not installed". Trade: the cursor is cleared before the item is picked up, a locked item is reported, and a trade also counts as complete when the item has left the bags although the game never said "Trade complete." (the real run of 2026-10-22 ended without that message); the game's info messages (the other player's name masked) and the accept flags of a run are kept in `tradetest` to see what the game really says. New files `core/Version.lua` and `ui/VersionWindow.lua`: a full game restart is needed.
- Award by trade and sessions in combat (build 0.0.14): every answer in the loot master's window has a Trade button; it opens the trade window with that raider (found in the group by name), picks the item up from the bags and puts it into the first free trade slot, and the loot master and the raider press Trade themselves (the addon never accepts); when the game says "Trade complete." the item shows as given. `/im trade <item number> [name]` does the same from the chat (without a name: the player targeted). Each step and the game's answer is kept in `IntrepidManagerDB.tradetest` (no names), because no real run has shown which of these calls an addon may make. A session can now be started in ordinary combat; only a boss fight, when the game blocks addon messages, still refuses. New file `game/Trade.lua`: a full game restart is needed.
- The answer window (Loot offered) closes by itself two seconds after every offer is over, when the time runs out or the loot master ends the session (build 0.0.13). `/im offers` opens it again.
- Window polish after the first look in the beta (build 0.0.12): the three council windows open in different places (session window top left, offer frame top right, bag picker top), the offer frame is as tall as its rows, raiders' names in the session window have the picture of their class beside them (the game's own `ClassIcon_` files, seen to draw), and the record keeps a timeline of offers, hellos, answers and the end (`council.log`, no names) to read real timings from.
- Council session windows (build 0.0.11): the loot master's window (`/im session`: the queue with pictures, Add from bags, the timer, Start and End, and the answers of the picked item with raiders' names in class colors), the bag picker (clickable list of the bags; the box Include grays and whites, which ticks itself when nothing better is there) and the raider's answer frame (opens by itself on an offer: a row per item with its tooltip, the time left and the buttons Best in slot, Upgrade, Off-spec and Pass, with a note). The offer now carries the item's name and quality and the hello the class. A new `clear` chrome in the kit (translucent dark panel, thin border) and a text fitter. Chat commands still work.
- Council session, slice 1 (build 0.0.10): the loot master queues items that are already looted (shift-clicked links, or a list of the bags with the trade time left) and starts a session with a timer; every raider with the addon is offered the items and answers each (Best in slot, Upgrade, Off-spec or Pass, with a note) before the timer ends; the loot master sees the answers. Chat commands: `/im session add|bags|bag|list|remove|start|results|end`, `/im respond`, `/im offers`. The pure engine (`core/Council`), a test wire format (`net/CouncilWire`, prefix `IMC1`), a sender queue that keeps to the game's allowance of 10 messages and then 1 a second per prefix (`net/Outbox`), the bag list (`game/Bags`) and the client side (`game/CouncilSession`). Windows follow in the next step.
